RL Arcade

Watch a Q-table learn Snake and Flappy Bird in your browser, see what it sees, then try to beat it yourself. Same games and state encodings as the notebooks.

Snake – tabular Q-learning on 11 yes/no features

The agent sees only: danger ahead/right/left, its heading, and which way the food is. That is at most 211 = 2048 table rows × 3 actions (straight, turn right, turn left).

Try this: (1) Watch agent before training – it goes straight into a wall. (2) Train 500 games, watch again. (3) Train another 1000: the score plateaus around 15–25 – watch how it dies: it curls into a pocket of its own body, because it only sees the three cells next to its head. (4) Guess first, then check: Reset, set γ = 0 (no thought for the future at all) and train 1500 games. Surprise – in our runs the myopic snake did better (test mean 20–22 vs 13–19 with γ = 0.9, four seeds). With only three cells of vision there is nothing to plan for: the immediate reward already says “eat” and “don't die”. (5) Play yourself with the arrow keys, WASD or the buttons – can you beat the table?

Flappy Bird – tabular Q-learning on a discretised state

Distance to the next pipe (6 boxes), gap centre − bird height (17 boxes), vertical speed (18 values): 1836 rows × 2 actions (glide, flap). Ties go to glide, and the table starts at zero.

Try this: (1) Train 1500 games (3 clicks) with the default death reward −10 and ε = 0, then watch. (2) Reset, set the death reward to −1 and train 1500 again: the bird barely learns – with ε = 0 it only tries flapping where gliding looks worse than 0, and a −1 crash is outweighed by ten frames of +0.1. (3) Keep −1 but set ε = 0.01: a little randomness rescues it. (4) Push ε to 0.1 – too much randomness: in our runs it scored lower on average than ε = 0.01. (5) Play yourself: Space, ↑ or a click/tap on the game flaps. Games are capped at 50 pipes.
