# On Imposter Syndrome in ML

ML is a very broad field. Nobody knows all of it: a recommender-systems researcher may know little about diffusion models, and an RL researcher may never have tuned XGBoost. The aim isn't to know everything; it's to have **solid foundations** plus **depth in one or two areas**, and to be able to learn the rest when you need it.

## Turn the feeling into evidence
1. **Self-assess honestly** with [[Masters Self-Assessment]]. Most people find they know far more than they feel they do. The gaps become a concrete list, not a vague fear.
2. **Build things from scratch**, using [[Implement From Scratch]]. If you can implement backprop, k-means and attention, you understand them. That is hard to argue with.
3. **Keep a "done" log.** Add a line here whenever you finish a course, project, paper or exam. Re-read it on bad days.
4. **Explain to someone else.** If you can teach it at a whiteboard, you know it. If you get stuck, you've found exactly what to study next.
5. **Read papers regularly**, using [[Paper Reading Template]] and [[Research Skills]]. After about 20 papers in one sub-area, you'll be able to tell good papers from weak ones.

## Useful reframes
- "I don't know X" → "I haven't learned X *yet*, and I know where to look" ([[00 - Learning Path]]).
- Feeling confused while reading papers is normal for everyone. Papers are compressed and often leave out the hard parts.
- Comparing your behind-the-scenes struggle to other people's polished results (LinkedIn, Twitter) is an unfair comparison.
- Being able to ask *good questions* is itself a skill. Seniors stand out by their questions more than by their answers.
